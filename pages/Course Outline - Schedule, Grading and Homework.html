Course Description: This one semester course provides an introduction to descriptive statistics, laws of probability, distributions of continuous and discrete random variables, inferential statistics, correlation and linear regression. This course rigorously develops statistical theory and is intended for those students who will continue on in applied disciplines or wish to pursue more statistics courses.

Prerequisite: MATH 1120 or equivalent.
Corequisite: MATH 1220 or equivalent.

Text: Devore, Probability and Statistics for Engineering and the Sciences, 9th edition with WebAssign.
It is recommended that you purchase your copy of the textbook from the College bookstore to ensure you get a valid WebAssign code.

✎ Explanation (added)

Time. 2:30 to 4:20 is 110 minutes, which is what the schedule's heading means by "two hour lectures". Office hours are the hour just before class in S3940, on the same days.

Why calculus is required. MATH 1120 and MATH 1220 are Calculus I and II. Chapters 1 to 3 (Lectures 1 to 10 on this site) use sums; from §4.1 on, probabilities of continuous random variables are areas under a density curve, so they are integrals, and expected values become integrals as well.

The description in the order of the schedule: descriptive statistics (Ch. 1), laws of probability (Ch. 2), discrete and continuous distributions (Ch. 3–5), inferential statistics (Ch. 6–9), correlation and linear regression (Ch. 12).

3Policies

Policies:

  1. Term tests will not be rescheduled, nor will there be any make-up examinations.
  2. If a student is unable to write an examination due to circumstances beyond their control, the instructor must be notified before the examination and the student will be expected to provide the instructor with acceptable supporting documentation as soon as possible.
  3. Final exams cannot be written early.
  4. Academic dishonesty will not be tolerated. Students should refer to the College Academic Integrity Policy for complete details of what constitutes academic dishonesty, and the consequences that may result from such behaviour.
    https://www.douglascollege.ca/programs-courses/explore-programs-courses/faculties/science-and-technology/mathematics/math-lab
  5. Calculator memories must be cleared at the start of all tests and exams. The use of other electronic devices or aids will not be permitted on these assessments.

4Attendance, homework, calculator, tutorials and the Math Lab

Attendance:

Generally speaking, attendance is related to performance. Students that attend lectures on a regular basis tend to stay on track, understand more, and perform better than students who miss lectures. It is College policy that any student who misses more than 30% of the scheduled lectures, or misses evaluations totaling more than 30% of the overall course grade, will be prohibited from writing the final exam and will receive a non-attendance grade (UN).

✎ Explanation (added)

What 30% is in numbers. The schedule has 22 lecture days, plus the two term-test days, for 24 class meetings. 30% of 22 is 6.6 and 30% of 24 is 7.2. If the test days are not counted, missing 7 lectures is already over the line; if they are, missing 8 is.

Evaluations. Missing one term test is 25% of the grade, so after that only 5 more percentage points of missed work (for example half of the tutorial marks) would push it over 30% and rule out the final.

Homework:

Weekly assignments will be completed using the publisher’s online homework system and will be due each Monday at 11:59pm. In addition to the graded online assignments, recommended homework questions from the textbook are included with the tentative lecture schedule on the next page. Written solutions to these recommended questions will not be collected or graded but students are expected to complete them in a timely fashion.

Calculator:

A calculator capable of performing basic statistical functions and dealing with common distributions is required for this course. The recommended calculator is a TI-83/84+ or equivalent. Calculators can be used on all forms of assessment. The statistical software package R will be used with some assignment questions, but you will not have access to R on exams.

✎ Explanation (added)

Homework. "The publisher's online homework system" is WebAssign (named under Text in section 1), due Mondays at 11:59 PM. The textbook questions are the right-hand column of the schedule below; each lecture page on this site lists them with page numbers in its "In the textbook" section and works one of them out.

Calculator. The TI-84 Plus CE is in the TI-84+ family, so it is the recommended calculator. So far the lecture pages have used 1-Var Stats, nPr, nCr, binompdf, binomcdf and poissoncdf; normalcdf and invNorm come with §4.3. Anything done in R for an assignment has to be done by calculator or by hand on a test.

Tutorials:

Weekly tutorial sessions are scheduled for this course. In the tutorials, you will have the opportunity to work on skills that will help you understand the material covered in class. Most tutorial sessions will involve group work that will be graded. The group work will account for 10% of your overall grade.

The Math Lab:

The Math Lab can be found in Room S3910. During open lab times, TA’s will be available to help with textbook problems and concepts covered in the course. Students are encouraged to take advantage of this resource. Please see the posted schedule.
